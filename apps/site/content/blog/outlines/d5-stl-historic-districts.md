## Outline

### Intro (75 words)
- Open with the misconception: a house being old doesn't make it eligible for historic tax credits
- Specific designation matters — National Register listing, Certified Local Historic District, or contributing structure status
- St. Louis has an unusually rich inventory of designated historic districts — and a robust state credit program to match
- Thesis: understanding which districts qualify — and what contributing structure status means — is the first step before starting any major rehabilitation

---

### H2: Two Types of Historic District Designation (150 words)
- **National Register Historic Districts:** Listed by the National Park Service through the State Historic Preservation Office. Qualifying structures can access both federal (income-producing) and Missouri (residential + income-producing) tax credits.
- **Certified Local Historic Districts:** Designated by local municipalities (in STL's case, often the city's Cultural Resources Office). These overlap with but are not identical to NR districts. Local designation may trigger design review requirements even without tax credit implications.
- The key question for tax credits: is your property a **contributing structure** within a NR district? Non-contributing structures (additions, buildings from the wrong era, substantially altered structures) do not qualify.
- How to check: Missouri SHPO's online database, the National Register nomination documents for your district, or Landmarks Association of St. Louis resources

---

### H2: City of St. Louis — Historic Districts (300 words)
Brief guide to each major STL City district — 2–3 sentences each, note credit eligibility:

- **Central West End:** NR-listed; large district; pre-1900 through early 20th century residential and commercial. Pocket doors, original millwork, staircases — common scope for wood refinishing. Eligible.
- **Lafayette Square:** One of STL's oldest restored neighborhoods. Victorian-era homes, many with original woodwork largely intact. Eligible.
- **Compton Heights:** Craftsman and Prairie style homes; built-ins, original cabinetry. Eligible.
- **Tower Grove East / Tower Grove South:** Dense urban residential; Victorian and early 20th century stock. Eligible.
- **Shaw:** Bungalows and two-flats; strong intact woodwork. Eligible.
- **Soulard:** STL's oldest surviving neighborhood; Federal and Greek Revival homes. Eligible; lead paint considerations common.
- **Maplewood:** Inner-ring suburb with strong NR district; bungalow and cottage stock. Eligible.
- Note: not exhaustive — check SHPO database for additional districts and individual listings

---

### H2: Inner Ring and County — What's Out There (150 words)
- **University City / Delmar Loop:** NR district covers portions of U City's residential core
- **Webster Groves:** Several NR-listed properties and small districts; individual listings more common than large district designations
- **Kirkwood:** Has NR-listed individual properties and some district-level designations
- **Clayton:** Some NR-listed properties; mix of residential and commercial historic significance
- West County suburbs (Chesterfield, Ballwin, Ladue, etc.): generally do not have large historic district designations; individual listing possible for exceptional properties
- Practical note: even without district-level designation, an individually NR-listed property qualifies for the same credits

---

### H2: What "Contributing Structure" Actually Means (150 words)
- Within a historic district, not every building automatically qualifies
- Contributing: a structure that was built during the period of significance and retains sufficient historic integrity
- Non-contributing: built outside the period of significance, or so substantially altered that it no longer reads as historic
- How to find out: the NR nomination document for your district lists contributing vs. non-contributing structures by address; SHPO can also verify
- Important for sellers and buyers: contributing status affects the property's tax credit eligibility and can affect resale positioning in a historic district
