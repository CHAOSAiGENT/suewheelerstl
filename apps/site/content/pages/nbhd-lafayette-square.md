## SECTION: Lafayette Square homes and their woodwork

The homes in Lafayette Square were built between the 1860s and the 1890s — Italianate, Second Empire, and Queen Anne styles that were the height of fashion in St. Louis's most prosperous decades. These are houses designed to impress, and the woodwork inside reflects that ambition.

Elaborate turned and carved spindles on the staircase. Heavy pocket door surrounds with raised molding and fluted pilasters. Wainscoting that runs the full height of the dining room. Front doors with multi-panel detail, heavy original hardware, and transoms above. Crown molding with ogee and dentil profiles that required skilled millwork to produce and skilled hands to restore.

Lafayette Square is a designated historic district, and many properties are individually significant. Homeowners here are typically serious about preservation — they bought in the neighborhood because of its architecture, and they want work done to a standard that respects what's there.

That also means they've sometimes had bad experiences. Contractors who didn't understand what they were looking at. Chemical dip-stripping that raised grain and softened profiles. Painters who covered original stained woodwork with latex because it was faster. We see the results of those decisions every time we work in the neighborhood.

## SECTION: What we refinish in Lafayette Square

Lafayette Square projects are often the most architecturally complex work we do. The woodwork in these homes was built by craftsmen; restoring it requires the same level of care.

**Pocket doors.** Lafayette Square homes frequently have multiple sets of pocket doors — sometimes on every floor. Most are original fir or oak with raised panel detail and ornate surrounds. Many have been painted over once, twice, or more. We remove each door from its pocket, hand-strip every surface, refinish to match the surrounding millwork, and reinstall. Original hardware is preserved wherever possible.

**Staircases.** Victorian staircases in Lafayette Square are among the most ornate in St. Louis residential architecture. Turned and carved spindles, heavy carved newel posts, continuous curved railings with return details. Each component is stripped by hand — the profiles on these pieces are destroyed by dipping, and machine sanding removes the fine detail. We strip and finish them the same way they were built: by hand.

**Front doors.** A Lafayette Square front door is a statement piece for the whole block. Solid wood, multi-panel, often with a fanlight transom. South and west-facing doors take year-round UV and weather damage. We remove, strip, and refinish in our shop for consistent results — and rehang on-site so the door operates exactly as it should.

**Victorian millwork and wainscoting.** The interior trim in Lafayette Square homes has heavier profiles than Craftsman or Colonial Revival. Floor-to-ceiling wainscoting, elaborate baseboards, picture rails, ornate door and window surrounds. When all of it has been painted over, restoring it room by room changes the character of the house fundamentally.

## SECTION: Restoring original woodwork vs. replacing it

The woodwork in Lafayette Square homes was built with old-growth fir and oak milled from trees that were already hundreds of years old. That wood is denser than anything available new — it takes finish differently, holds detail more sharply, and is more dimensionally stable. There is no substitute for it.

The profiles on original Victorian millwork were custom-milled at local shops that no longer exist. Reproducing them requires custom millwork at significant expense — and even then, new wood won't have the density, character, or figure of what's already in the house.

The case for restoration is almost always overwhelming: strip what's there, repair what needs repair, apply a finish that lets the material show. The result is woodwork that looks as it was intended to look when the house was built.

## SECTION: Lead paint and pre-1978 homes in Lafayette Square

Lafayette Square homes don't just predate 1978 — they predate 1900. The woodwork in these houses has been finished and refinished multiple times over 130-plus years. Lead paint is present in virtually every original finish layer, and in many cases in every layer since.

Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified under the RRP rule. Proper containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are standard on every Lafayette Square project. The complexity of the woodwork here means the refinishing process disturbs significant amounts of finish material — which makes certification even more important, not less.

If you're planning a major restoration scope in Lafayette Square, make sure every contractor touching woodwork can show you EPA Certified Lead Renovator documentation. It's federal law and it protects your family.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: My pocket doors are stuck inside the wall and the hardware is broken. Can they be restored?**
In most cases, yes. We extract the doors from the pocket, assess the pocket frame and hardware, and make repairs as needed. Original pocket door hardware from this era is usually castings of brass or iron — frequently repairable or replaceable with period-appropriate hardware. The door itself is typically in better shape than the hardware.

**Q: Some of the spindles on my staircase are damaged or missing. Can you replace just those?**
We can. Replacement turned spindles can be sourced or custom-turned to match the existing profile. We match the stain and finish so replacements are not visible in the finished result.

**Q: I have wainscoting that runs floor-to-ceiling in two rooms. How do you approach a scope that large?**
Room by room, systematically. We strip in place — wainscoting can't be removed without damage to the plaster behind it. We contain the area, work methodically from top to bottom, and apply finish with care for the profiles and transitions.

**Q: My front door faces south and the finish is completely failed. How often will I need to refinish it?**
A properly applied exterior finish on a south-facing door will typically last 3–7 years depending on how much direct sun it gets and how well the door is sealed at the bottom and sides. We offer a maintenance coat — Perk Up & Protect — for doors that are in good shape but need refreshing, which extends the interval between full refinishes.

**Q: Do you work in Lafayette Square often?**
Yes, regularly. We know the neighborhood's housing stock and the particular challenges it presents.
