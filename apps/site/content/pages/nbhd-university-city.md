## SECTION: University City homes and their woodwork

University City developed rapidly between 1910 and 1940, when Tudor Revival, Colonial Revival, American Foursquare, and Craftsman bungalow were the dominant residential styles in St. Louis's inner-ring suburbs. The result is a neighborhood with unusual architectural consistency — and interiors built with high-quality wood as a defining feature.

Tudor Revivals in U-City have casement windows with thick wood surrounds, built-in bookcases flanking fireplaces, and staircases with heavy newel posts and substantial railings. Colonial Revivals have paneled wainscoting, wide-plank flooring, and detailed door surrounds. Craftsman bungalows — common especially in the blocks closest to the city — have stained oak millwork throughout: built-in bookshelves, buffets, window seats, and kitchen cabinetry built to last.

What connects all of these styles is original wood that was installed to last and is still in place. University City residents tend to know what they have — and when it needs work, they research who does it right.

Sue Wheeler has worked extensively in University City throughout her career.

## SECTION: What we refinish in University City

University City projects span the full range of architectural woodwork. The most common work here:

**Craftsman kitchen cabinet refinishing** — Original Craftsman-era kitchen cabinetry in U-City bungalows is typically solid oak with recessed panel doors. These aren't cheap boxes — they're built furniture that belongs in the house. We strip and refinish rather than paint over, preserving the grain and the patina that makes them look original. The result lasts for decades.

**Built-in bookshelves and millwork** — The Craftsman built-in is everywhere in U-City: flanking fireplaces, lining hallways, built into dining rooms as buffets. When the finish goes flat or someone painted them, the fix is hand-stripping and careful refinishing. We do this work in place, with full masking to protect surrounding surfaces.

**Staircase refinishing** — Tudor and Colonial Revival staircases in U-City homes tend toward the substantial: heavy newel posts, continuous railings, and spindles that range from simple to turned and carved. We refinish the full staircase — treads, risers, spindles, newel post, and railing — stripped and done together so the finish reads as one piece.

**Door refinishing** — Both the heavy exterior doors typical of Tudor architecture and the panel interior doors throughout these homes are frequently original and worth preserving. We remove, strip, and refinish in our shop, then reinstall.

## SECTION: Preserving original woodwork

The Craftsman and Tudor woodwork in University City homes was built with old-growth oak and fir — wood that's denser and more stable than anything available today. Stripping and refinishing it preserves that material. Replacing it with new millwork costs more, looks worse, and removes something irreplaceable from the house.

Sue's approach is always to assess what's actually there before recommending a path. Sometimes a light clean-up and topcoat is all that's needed. Sometimes a full strip is the right call. The goal is always the result that makes the wood look the way it should and lasts as long as possible.

## SECTION: Lead paint and older homes in University City

Most University City housing stock predates 1978. EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) regulations apply to refinishing work that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes. A Certified Lead Renovator is required.

Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified. Proper containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are included on every applicable project — not add-ons.

## FAQ

**Do you work with Craftsman homes specifically?**
Yes. Craftsman-era woodwork — stained oak millwork, built-in cabinetry, built-in bookshelves — is a significant part of what we do. University City bungalows are well represented in our project history.

**Can original kitchen cabinets really be saved if the finish is worn or damaged?**
In most cases, yes. Original Craftsman-era kitchen cabinets are solid wood — they can be stripped and refinished repeatedly. The question is whether the wood itself is sound, which Sue will assess during the estimate.

**What does a staircase refinishing project involve?**
We do the full scope: treads, risers, spindles, newel post, and railing. Everything is stripped to bare wood, repaired as needed, stained to match (or a new color if you're changing it), and finished with a durable topcoat. We work in the home with proper masking and containment.

**How long does it take?**
Project timelines vary by scope. A single door is typically a week turnaround (shop work). A full staircase or kitchen cabinet job is usually scheduled over several days on-site. Sue will give you a specific timeline during the estimate.

**Is the estimate really free?**
Yes. Sue will come to your home, look at the wood, and tell you what it needs and what it costs — no charge, no obligation.
