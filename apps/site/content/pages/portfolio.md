## SECTION: Staircases

**H2:** Staircases

Original staircases in historic St. Louis homes were built from hard species — white oak, yellow pine, old-growth fir — with hand-turned newel posts and balusters that modern millwork can approximate but can't duplicate. When they're restored rather than replaced, the result is a staircase that looks precisely right because it was made for the house.

The projects below include full staircase refinishing, individual component work (newel posts, handrails, treads), and staircases where the original wood had been buried under paint.

## SECTION: Kitchen Cabinets

**H2:** Kitchen Cabinets

Kitchens are where most homeowners first realize what refinishing can do. A kitchen that looks dated or worn typically has cabinets that are structurally sound but carrying a finish that's 20 or 30 years old. Strip it back to bare wood, repair what needs repairing, and apply a new finish — the cabinets look new because everything visible is new.

The projects below include stained cabinets, painted cabinets, color changes, and cabinets with butler's pantries refinished as part of the same project.

## SECTION: Architectural Woodwork

**H2:** Architectural Woodwork

Wainscoting, crown molding, built-in hutches and bookcases, mantels, door casings, window trim, chair rail — the in-place wood that gives a room its character. This is some of the most satisfying work because the transformation is total: a room that looked tired and layered with old paint becomes what it was always meant to be.

Most of these projects are in CWE, Benton Park, Compton Heights, and Lafayette Square homes from 1890 to 1930. Original material, restored in place.

## SECTION: Perk Up & Protect

**H2:** Perk Up & Protect

Not every surface needs a full strip-and-refinish. For architectural woodwork that's structurally sound and holding its finish — just looking dull or worn — a PUP maintenance coat gets the surfaces looking right again without the time and cost of starting over.

These projects show what a maintenance coat accomplishes on surfaces that are in good shape but needed a refresh. Less dramatic than a full restoration, but exactly the right service for the situation.

## SECTION: A note on photos.

Wood refinishing photographs imperfectly. Most of our best work — a matched stain on a 90-year-old built-in, a front door that looks like it was installed last week — doesn't show fully in a photo. The grain depth, the sheen, the way the finish sits on old-growth wood: these are things you notice standing in front of the piece.

If you want to see work that matches your specific situation — a particular wood species, finish type, or level of existing wear — call Sue. She can describe what she's seen in situations like yours and what the result typically looks like. That conversation is free and more useful than any photo.
