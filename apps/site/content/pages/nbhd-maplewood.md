## SECTION: Maplewood homes and their woodwork

Maplewood was built out primarily between 1900 and 1930, which makes it one of the earliest and most architecturally coherent suburbs in St. Louis County. Craftsman bungalows and American Foursquares dominate, with Folk Victorian cottages interspersed throughout — compact homes on tight lots, built with real materials and an attention to craftsmanship that doesn't exist in new construction.

The interior woodwork reflects the era: original fir and oak floors, simple but solid Craftsman trim, front doors that were built to last a century, and kitchen cabinetry that predates the disposable-cabinet era by decades. In the smaller galley kitchens typical of Maplewood bungalows, the original cabinets fit the space perfectly — and they're solid wood.

Maplewood also has an active market. New owners regularly buy these homes, discover original woodwork under layers of paint, and decide whether to restore it. Almost always, the right answer is to restore it — it's one of the things that made the house worth buying.

Sue Wheeler has worked throughout Maplewood for decades.

## SECTION: What we refinish in Maplewood

Maplewood projects center on the features that define the neighborhood's housing stock. The most common work here:

**Kitchen cabinet refinishing** — Original Craftsman-era kitchen cabinetry in Maplewood bungalows is solid wood with recessed panel doors — worth saving. When the finish is worn or peeling, or someone painted over the stained oak, we strip and refinish rather than replace. In many cases a PUP (paint-over-painted) process is appropriate for cabinets already in good structural shape. Sue will assess what's actually there and recommend the right path.

**Door refinishing** — Front doors on Maplewood homes are frequently original and in better structural shape than their finish suggests. We remove, strip, and refinish in our shop, then reinstall. Interior panel doors throughout these homes are also common candidates for refinishing when they've been painted.

**Staircase refinishing** — Craftsman-era staircases in Maplewood bungalows are typically straight-run, clean-lined, and built with fir or oak treads and simple turned spindles. When new owners pull up carpet and find original hardwood, a staircase restoration is often the first significant project. We do the full scope: treads, risers, spindles, newel post, and railing — stripped and refinished together.

**Built-in buffets and dining room millwork** — Many Maplewood Foursquares and larger bungalows have original built-in buffets in the dining room. When these have been painted over, hand-stripping restores both the grain and the built-in's character.

## SECTION: Preserving original woodwork

Maplewood's housing stock was built with old-growth fir and oak — wood that's denser and more dimensionally stable than modern lumber. Replacing it costs more and produces a worse result. Refinishing preserves the material, maintains the character of the house, and lasts for decades when done correctly.

Sue's estimate process starts with an assessment of what's actually there: the wood species, the finish condition, what's under any paint, whether the wood is structurally sound. The recommendation follows from that — not from a preset menu of services.

## SECTION: Lead paint and older homes in Maplewood

Most Maplewood housing stock predates 1978. EPA RRP regulations apply to refinishing work that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes — a Certified Lead Renovator is required.

Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified. Containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are part of every applicable project.

## FAQ

**Can you refinish kitchen cabinets that have been painted over?**
Often yes. If the underlying wood is sound — which it usually is with Craftsman-era solid wood cabinets — the paint can be stripped and the original finish restored. In some cases a PUP (paint-over-painted) refinish is a better option. Sue will assess the cabinets during the estimate and tell you which approach makes sense.

**What's involved in refinishing a Craftsman staircase?**
We refinish the full staircase as a unit: treads, risers, spindles, newel post, and railing — all stripped to bare wood, repaired as needed, stained, and finished together. The result looks like one piece of work, not a patchwork.

**My front door is in rough shape. Is it worth refinishing or should I replace it?**
Original Maplewood front doors are almost always worth refinishing. They're solid wood and built better than modern replacements. If the wood itself is sound, a refinish will look better and last longer than a new door at the same price point. Sue will assess it during the estimate.

**How long do refinishing projects take?**
Door refinishing is typically a week (shop work). Staircase or kitchen cabinet projects are usually scheduled over several days on-site. Sue will give you a specific timeline.

**Is the estimate free?**
Yes. Sue comes to your home, looks at the wood, and tells you what it needs and what it costs — no charge, no obligation.
