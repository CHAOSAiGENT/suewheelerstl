## SECTION: Clayton homes and their woodwork

Clayton's residential neighborhoods developed primarily between 1910 and 1950 — Colonial Revival, Tudor Revival, and American Traditional homes that were built for professional families who expected quality and permanence. These are not the ornate Victorian homes of Lafayette Square or the Craftsman bungalows of Shaw. They're something different: well-proportioned, classically detailed, built with solid materials that were meant to last.

The kitchen cabinets in a 1935 Clayton Colonial are typically face-frame construction with solid wood doors — raised panel, flat panel, or simple shaker profiles. The finish has aged, the color may have darkened or gone flat, but the underlying material is almost always superior to what you'd install new. A front door in Moorlands or Carrswold is likely solid mahogany, walnut, or fir — heavy, well-hung, and worth maintaining properly. Interior doors throughout are frequently solid wood with traditional panel profiles.

Clayton homeowners tend to be busy, research-oriented, and quality-focused. They want work done correctly and they don't want to manage a contractor through every step. They've also priced the alternatives. Replacing solid wood kitchen cabinets — even with good-quality new material — costs many times more than refinishing and produces a result that lacks the character of what's already there.

## SECTION: What we refinish in Clayton

Clayton projects center on the pieces that make the most visible difference in a well-maintained home: the kitchen, the front entry, and the formal rooms.

**Kitchen cabinet refinishing.** This is the most common scope we see in Clayton, and for good reason. Original kitchen cabinetry in Clayton's pre-war homes is typically solid wood with built-in character that new cabinets can't replicate — frame and panel construction, graduated shelf heights, hardware that was made to last. We strip by hand, repair any damage to doors or frames, and refinish to either restore the original tone or update it. The result looks like a new kitchen without replacing anything.

**Front door refinishing.** Clayton front doors are statements of the home's quality — and they take a beating from weather, sun, and decades of varying maintenance. A door that's been allowed to go gray and dull can look like it needs replacing when what it actually needs is stripping back to bare wood and a fresh finish. We remove the door, do the full scope in our shop, and rehang it — usually in one visit.

**Interior doors and formal room woodwork.** Traditional Colonial and Tudor homes in Clayton typically have solid wood interior doors — six-panel or four-panel profiles — and formal rooms with door and window surrounds that have real presence. When the finish has gone flat or uneven across the house, we address it systematically.

**Perk Up & Protect.** For Clayton homes where the woodwork is in good overall condition but looking worn, our maintenance coat scope refreshes the finish without requiring a full strip. Kitchen cabinet doors that have lost their sheen, interior woodwork that's dull but sound — this is the right scope for wood that's been well cared for and just needs attention.

## SECTION: Restoring original woodwork vs. replacing it

Clayton homeowners often arrive at this conversation after getting a replacement quote. Cabinet replacement for a mid-sized Clayton kitchen runs $25,000 to $60,000 or more depending on material quality. A full kitchen cabinet refinishing scope runs a fraction of that — and the result, when the original cabinets are solid wood, is genuinely better. New semi-custom cabinets at that price point are typically plywood or MDF boxes with wood veneer fronts. Original Clayton cabinetry is solid wood throughout.

The same logic applies to doors and millwork. The material that's already in a Clayton home is better than most contemporary replacement material at any price point. It's worth maintaining.

## SECTION: Lead paint and pre-1978 homes in Clayton

Clayton's pre-war housing stock — homes built before 1940 — all predate the 1978 lead paint ban. Any refinishing work on original woodwork in these homes should be treated as lead-paint work until tested otherwise.

Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified under the RRP rule. Containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are standard on every applicable project. For kitchen cabinet work especially — where the scope involves extensive finish disturbance in a high-use room — proper lead-safe procedure matters for your family's health.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: My kitchen cabinets are original to a 1940 Colonial. Are they worth refinishing or should I just replace them?**
In almost every case, original solid wood cabinets from that era are worth refinishing. The construction quality is genuinely superior to contemporary semi-custom at equivalent price points. We'll confirm at the estimate — and we'll tell you honestly if we find anything that changes that answer.

**Q: Can you change the color of my kitchen cabinets, not just restore the original finish?**
Yes. Color change is a standard part of our kitchen cabinet scope. We strip to bare wood, which eliminates the original color entirely, and apply whatever stain and topcoat you've chosen. We provide samples and walk you through the options.

**Q: How long does a kitchen cabinet refinishing take?**
A typical Clayton kitchen takes 3–5 days. We remove all doors and drawers and take them to our shop; the box frames are done in place. You'll have limited kitchen access during the scope — we'll walk through the logistics before we start.

**Q: My front door is solid mahogany and the finish is completely failed. Can it be saved?**
Almost certainly yes. Mahogany is extremely stable and holds finish well. Failed finish on a mahogany door is a refinishing job, not a replacement job — assuming the wood itself is sound, which it almost always is on a door that was protected by proper overhang.

**Q: I don't know who last worked on my woodwork or what they used. Does that matter?**
It affects the stripping process — some finishes are harder to remove than others — but it doesn't change the outcome. We assess finish condition at the estimate and factor it into the scope. We've stripped every finish combination you can imagine.
