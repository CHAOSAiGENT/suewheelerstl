## SECTION: Painted. Stained. Color-changed. We do all three.

**H2:** Whatever your cabinets look like now, we can change that.

Original wood cabinets in St. Louis homes come in every condition: painted over in the 1970s, stained with a color that felt right in 2003 but doesn't anymore, or solid and structurally sound but dull and worn from 20 years of daily use. In each case, the answer is the same — strip the existing finish back to bare wood, prepare the surface properly, and apply the right finish for how a kitchen actually gets used.

What we don't do: spray a new coat over an old one. That's not refinishing. It's painting over a problem. Within a few years the old finish telegraphs through, adhesion fails, and you're back where you started — or worse.

Every cabinet project starts at bare wood. That's what "refinishing" means.

## SECTION: Why refinish instead of replace?

**H2:** New cabinets cost $15,000–$50,000. Refinishing costs a fraction of that.

**The money.** New kitchen cabinetry — real wood, installed, with hardware — runs $15,000 to $50,000 or more depending on scope. We've had homeowners call us after getting a replacement quote and nearly falling over. Refinishing the existing cabinet boxes and doors costs significantly less, and you end up with wood that's been on your walls for decades and fits your kitchen exactly.

**The wood.** Most kitchen cabinets in pre-1978 St. Louis homes were built from old-growth lumber — slower-growing, tighter-grained wood that doesn't exist commercially anymore. That wood is denser, harder, and holds finish better than what goes into new cabinetry today. When you replace original cabinets, you're trading down on material quality.

**The fit.** Original cabinets were built to the specific dimensions of the kitchen. Replacement cabinets are close but never quite right — there's always a gap somewhere, a trim piece that doesn't align, a proportion that looks a little off. Original cabinets, properly restored, simply fit better.

**The butler's pantry.** Have one? Same process, same result. Butler's pantry cabinetry is identical in scope to a kitchen project — we treat it as part of the same job.

## SECTION: Is your kitchen a full refinish or a Perk Up & Protect candidate?

**H2:** Some kitchens need a full strip. Some just need a refresh.

**Strip & Refinish** is a complete restoration. Existing finish stripped to bare wood, any repairs made, new stain (if applicable), new polyurethane topcoat. For cabinets with failing finish, significant wear, a color change, or any paint applied over stain. This is the right call for most of the kitchens we see.

**Perk Up & Protect (PUP)** is a maintenance coat. Wet sand to level light surface damage, color refresh if needed, fresh topcoat over a solid existing base. For cabinets that are structurally sound and holding their finish but looking dull or lightly worn. Faster, less expensive, no chemical stripping.

You don't self-select — we assess your cabinets at the estimate and tell you which service makes sense. If your cabinets are a PUP candidate, we'll say so. If they need a full refinish, we'll explain why.

## SECTION: How kitchen cabinet refinishing works

**H2:** What actually happens between your estimate and the finished result.

**1. Assessment and estimate**
We look at the cabinets — finish type, wood species, condition, any damage. We give you a written estimate with real scope and a realistic timeline.

**2. Door and drawer front removal**
Most projects use our board-up method: we remove the cabinet doors and drawer fronts, transport them to the shop, and work in a controlled environment. Cabinet boxes stay in place. This keeps dust and fumes out of your kitchen and allows proper cure conditions.

**3. Hand-stripping**
Every piece is stripped by hand — no dip tank. This preserves the grain, the profiles, and the integrity of the wood. It takes longer. It's worth it.

**4. Repairs**
Dings, gouges, cracks, loose joints — addressed before finishing. We use appropriate materials for each situation.

**5. Stain (if applicable)**
We match your existing stain or help you select a new color. Stain is applied and wiped to the correct tone before the topcoat goes on.

**6. Finish**
Oil-based or water-based polyurethane selected for kitchen use. Multiple coats, sanded between coats. A kitchen cabinet finish needs to handle heat, humidity, cleaning products, and daily use for years — we apply it accordingly.

**7. Reinstallation**
Doors and drawer fronts returned and rehung. Hardware reinstalled. Your kitchen the way you left it — except the cabinets look 30 years newer.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: How long does kitchen cabinet refinishing take?**
A: Most projects run 1–3 weeks from pickup to reinstallation, depending on door count, finish type, and repair scope. We give you a specific timeline with your estimate.

**Q: Can you match my existing cabinet stain?**
A: Usually, yes. Stain matching is part of what we do. We'll assess the existing tone and get as close as possible — and we'll tell you honestly if an exact match isn't achievable before we start.

**Q: Will my cabinets look like new?**
A: If the wood is solid and we're starting from bare wood, the result is a finish that looks new because it is new. The wood underneath is original — in most pre-1978 homes, that means tighter grain and better material than you'd get from new cabinetry.

**Q: Do you do just the doors, or the boxes too?**
A: We do both, scoped to what each element needs. Most projects involve refinishing the cabinet doors and drawer fronts in the shop and addressing the cabinet boxes in place. We scope it at the estimate.

**Q: How much does kitchen cabinet refinishing cost?**
A: It depends on door and drawer front count, finish type, condition, and any repairs needed. We give free estimates — call (314) 367-6054. We'll give you a realistic range before you commit to anything.

**Q: Do I need to empty my cabinets?**
A: For door and drawer front removal, you don't need to empty the boxes — just clear the shelves immediately inside the doors being removed. We'll let you know exactly what to prepare at estimate time.
