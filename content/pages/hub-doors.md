## SECTION: The Board-Up Method

**H2:** We take the door to the shop. _Not the shop to the door._

Most contractors refinish doors in place — which means stripping chemicals, fumes, and sanding dust in your hallway. We do it differently.

For the majority of door projects, we remove the door from its hinges, transport it to our shop, strip and finish it in a controlled environment, then return and hang it. The result is better — controlled temperature and humidity produce a more consistent cure, and the board-up process allows complete access to every edge and profile. No fumes lingering in your house. No sanding dust on your floors.

It also means the disruption is minimal. You're without a door for a few days, not a few weeks of in-place work.

On-premise work is available when removal isn't practical — very large doors, built-in frame situations, or cases where removal would cause damage.

## SECTION: Service Options

**H2:** Strip & Refinish or _Perk Up & Protect?_

**Strip & Refinish (S&R)**
Full restoration. Strip to bare wood, repair any damage, stain if needed, finish with polyurethane. For doors with failing finish, UV damage, deep wear, or color change. This is the right choice for most doors that haven't been properly finished in years.

**Perk Up & Protect (PUP)**
Maintenance coat. Light scuff-sand and fresh finish over a sound existing base. For doors that are structurally intact and holding finish, but looking dull or lightly worn. Faster, less expensive, and not right for every door. We'll tell you which applies to yours.

## SECTION: Lead Paint & Pre-1978 Doors

**H2:** Most St. Louis doors have _lead paint._ We handle it correctly.

If your home was built before 1978 — and in St. Louis, that's the large majority of older homes in the city — the paint on your doors almost certainly contains lead. Stripping those doors without EPA-certified lead-safe protocols generates lead dust in the space your family walks through every day.

Sue Wheeler is an EPA Certified Lead Renovator. Every door project on a pre-1978 home is handled with proper containment, HEPA filtration, wet-sanding methods, and documented cleanup. You receive a completion record for your property file.

This isn't compliance language. It's the difference between work that's done safely and work that isn't.

## SECTION: FAQ BLOCK

**Q: How long does door refinishing take?**
A: A single door typically takes 2–4 days from pickup to reinstallation. Larger projects — front door with frame, or a full interior door set — run longer. We give you a specific timeline with your estimate.

**Q: Can you match the stain on my other woodwork?**
A: Usually. Stain matching across existing millwork is standard for interior door projects. We assess the existing tones and work to match closely. If an exact match isn't achievable — original finishes change color over decades — we tell you before starting.

**Q: Will my front door hold up to weather after refinishing?**
A: Yes, if finished correctly. Exterior doors need UV-resistant polyurethane and proper prep. We finish front doors for the exposure they face. How long it holds depends on orientation and overhang — a south-facing door in full sun needs more frequent attention than a covered north-facing one. We'll tell you what to expect for your specific door.

**Q: Do you refinish just the door slab or the frame too?**
A: Both, when possible. A refinished slab next to an untouched frame looks wrong. We scope accordingly.

**Q: Do you work on pocket doors?**
A: Yes. Pocket doors in historic St. Louis homes are pulled out, worked on, and returned. We've done plenty of them in Central West End and Lafayette Square homes.
