## SECTION: Benton Park homes and their woodwork

Benton Park is one of St. Louis's oldest continuously inhabited neighborhoods. The homes here were built primarily in the 1870s, 1880s, and 1890s — Italianate cottages, Folk Victorian row houses, Queen Anne details tucked onto modest facades. These aren't grand mansions. They're dense urban houses built with genuine craft: original doors with detailed panel profiles, narrow staircases with turned spindles and solid newel posts, dining rooms with built-in hutches that were standard in working-class homes of the period.

What makes Benton Park different from higher-profile preservation neighborhoods is the mix of residents: longtime owners who have cared for these houses for decades, and a newer wave of homeowners who bought a "fixer" and are discovering what's underneath the paint for the first time. In both cases, the woodwork is worth saving — and in many cases it's in better condition than it looks.

The neighborhood's proximity to Cherokee Street and its strong preservation culture mean that Benton Park homeowners tend to understand the value of what they have. They want it done right, not just done.

## SECTION: What we refinish in Benton Park

Benton Park projects often start with one piece — a front door that's been painted shut, or a dining room hutch that someone always meant to strip — and expand from there once the homeowner sees what's possible.

**Original Victorian doors.** The doors in Benton Park's Italianate and Folk Victorian homes are frequently original — solid wood, raised panel, still hanging on their original hinges. Many have been painted over so many times the panel detail has softened. We remove them, hand-strip every face and edge in our shop, and rehang them. The difference is immediate.

**Narrow staircases.** Benton Park's row houses and cottages have staircases sized for the floor plan: narrower than a CWE grand hall, but often with original spindle work and solid newel posts that are worth restoring. We strip and refinish treads, risers, spindles, and newels by hand — the only method that preserves the tight profiles of Victorian-era spindle turning.

**Built-in hutches and dining room millwork.** The dining room built-ins in Benton Park homes are one of the neighborhood's best-kept secrets. Built-in hutches with glass fronts, plate rails above, simple but well-made detail. Most have been painted over. Under that paint is typically solid oak or chestnut in good condition. We hand-strip them in place and refinish to match the room.

**Window and door surrounds.** Victorian interior trim in Benton Park has a distinctive profile — heavier than Craftsman, with more ornamental detail — and it runs through every room. When it's painted over, the house loses its character. When it's restored, the rooms look like themselves again.

## SECTION: Restoring original woodwork vs. replacing it

In a Benton Park home from the 1880s, the woodwork is irreplaceable in a literal sense. The species aren't available new. The profiles were milled locally and no longer exist. The tight grain that comes from old-growth trees simply cannot be replicated with contemporary lumber.

When a homeowner in Benton Park asks about replacing a built-in hutch or a set of original doors with something new, the honest answer is: you won't get close to what you have. You'll spend more and end up with less. Restoration — stripping back to the original wood, repairing any damage, applying a proper finish — is the right answer almost every time.

The exception is rot or structural failure. We'll tell you at the estimate if we find that. We don't push restoration when replacement is genuinely the right call.

## SECTION: Lead paint and pre-1978 homes in Benton Park

Every home in Benton Park predates 1978 — most predate 1920. Lead paint is present in virtually every original finish layer on every piece of woodwork. Stripping that woodwork without proper precautions creates a serious health hazard.

The EPA's RRP rule requires a Certified Lead Renovator for any refinishing work that disturbs lead-containing materials in a pre-1978 home. Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified. Proper containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are standard on every Benton Park job — not optional add-ons.

If you're a newer homeowner who just bought in Benton Park, this is especially important to know before you hire anyone for woodwork. Ask for certification documentation. A contractor who can't produce it isn't legal for this work.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: I bought a Benton Park house as a fixer and the woodwork is in rough shape. Where do I start?**
Start with the front door and the staircase — they're the most visible and they set the tone for everything else. Call for a free estimate and we'll walk through the whole house with you, identify what's worth restoring, and give you a clear picture of scope and cost. No pressure to do it all at once.

**Q: My dining room hutch has been painted white. Is the wood underneath worth saving?**
Usually yes. Most built-in hutches in Benton Park are solid oak or chestnut — dense, tight-grained, and typically in better condition than the exterior paint suggests. We've stripped hundreds of these. The wood underneath is almost always a pleasant surprise.

**Q: Can you work on original Victorian spindles without damaging them?**
Yes — but only by hand. Machine stripping would destroy the profiles. We strip by hand using methods appropriate to the profile and the finish condition. It takes longer but it's the only way to preserve the detail.

**Q: Do you do small jobs, or is there a minimum scope?**
We do single-door jobs. If you have one door that needs refinishing, we'll do it. Larger projects are obviously more efficient, but we don't require a minimum.

**Q: How does your pricing work?**
Every estimate is free and given in person after Sue has seen the wood. We don't publish prices because scope and condition vary too much — a door that's been painted twice is a different job than one that's been painted six times. You'll get a straight number before any work begins.
