### H2: The math on commercial wood maintenance.

A bar top refinish that costs $3,000 and lasts five years is $600 per year of service life. A bar top that gets ignored for eight years, fails completely, needs a more intensive restoration, and costs $6,000 — that's $750 per year, and you also dealt with a failing finish for the last three years of that cycle. Guests noticed. Staff noticed. It cost you before it cost you money.

Regular, scheduled maintenance keeps finishes in the window where a light prep and fresh topcoat is all that's needed. Once a finish has failed — peeling, gone tacky, chipped through to bare wood — the project scope jumps significantly. The difference between a maintenance coat and a full restoration is usually 3–4x in both time and cost.

A maintenance contract locks in the schedule and the price before either of those escalate.

### H2: What a maintenance contract covers.

We offer annual and multi-year maintenance agreements for commercial wood surfaces. A typical contract covers:

- **Scheduled condition assessment** — We inspect your wood surfaces at the start of each contract cycle and document current condition. This gives you a baseline and flags anything that needs attention before it becomes a full project.
- **Perk Up & Protect maintenance coat** — Light prep and fresh polyurethane topcoat on surfaces within the maintenance window. Extends the finish life without the cost and downtime of a full Strip & Refinish.
- **Priority scheduling** — Contract clients get first access to our schedule, including preferred off-hours and dark day slots.
- **Loyalty pricing** — Multi-year contract pricing is lower per-service than individual project pricing.
- **Documentation** — Condition reports at each visit. Useful for capital planning and for properties with preservation or compliance requirements.

### H2: When a maintenance contract makes sense.

A maintenance contract is the right structure for:

- **High-volume bars and restaurants** — Bar tops and tabletops on busy operations need attention every 2–3 years. A contract removes the decision-making from the cycle.
- **Hotels and event venues** — Lobby and event space woodwork is maintained on a rolling schedule alongside other property upkeep. Contracts align with annual capital budgets.
- **Multi-location operators** — Operators with more than one location benefit from a single point of contact and consolidated scheduling across properties.
- **Historic buildings with compliance requirements** — Properties that require EPA-certified and documentation-grade work on a recurring basis benefit from a contractor relationship that already understands the requirements.

### H2: What a contract doesn't cover.

A maintenance contract covers scheduled surface maintenance on surfaces that are within the maintenance window — surfaces with a solid finish base that needs refreshing. It doesn't cover full Strip & Refinish on surfaces that have failed, structural repairs, or emergency work outside the contract schedule. Those are scoped separately.

We assess condition at the start of each contract term and tell you clearly what's covered under maintenance and what would require separate project scope.

**Q: How often should a restaurant bar top be maintained?**
A: For a high-volume operation, every 2–3 years is typical. Moderate-volume operations may go 3–4 years. A lot depends on the finish specification, cleaning chemicals in use, and the physical volume on the surface. We assess and advise at the start of each contract cycle.

**Q: What's the difference between a maintenance coat and a full refinish?**
A: A maintenance coat (Perk Up & Protect) is light prep and a fresh topcoat on a finish that's still structurally sound. A full refinish (Strip & Refinish) takes the surface back to bare wood. Maintenance coats are faster, less expensive, and less disruptive — they're appropriate when the base finish is still holding. Full refinishing is required when the finish has failed or significant damage is present.

**Q: Can we add surfaces to the contract over time?**
A: Yes. Contracts can be structured to add surfaces as you expand or as other areas reach their maintenance window. We update the scope and pricing when that happens.

**Q: Do contracts work for multi-location operators?**
A: Yes — and it's often where a contract structure provides the most value. Consolidated scheduling, consistent documentation, and single-point-of-contact coordination across locations. Call us to discuss how to structure that.
