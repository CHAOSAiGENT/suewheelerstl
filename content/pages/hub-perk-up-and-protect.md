## SECTION: There's a service between "do nothing" and "full strip."

**H2:** Most refinishers will strip everything down to bare wood. PUP is different.

When architectural woodwork starts looking dull, lightly worn, or just tired — homeowners typically face two options: ignore it and watch it degrade, or pay for a full strip-and-refinish project. For woodwork with a failing base, that full refinish is exactly right. But for surfaces that are structurally sound and holding their finish? Stripping everything off and starting over is more than the situation requires.

Perk Up & Protect fills that gap. It's a maintenance coat: wet sand the surface to level any light wear and open the existing finish, refresh the color if needed, apply a fresh polyurethane topcoat over the existing base. No harsh chemical stripping. No sending doors off to a shop for weeks. Faster turnaround, lower cost, and less disruption.

No one else in St. Louis offers this as a dedicated standalone service. Most refinishers strip by default. PUP is the right call when the base doesn't need it.

## SECTION: What PUP actually involves.

**H2:** Three steps. No chemical stripping. No starting from bare wood.

**Step 1: Wet sand**
The existing finish is wet-sanded to level any light surface damage — minor scratches, small worn spots, surface irregularities — and to create mechanical adhesion for the new topcoat. This is not stripping. The existing finish base stays in place.

**Step 2: Color refresh (if needed)**
If the finish has faded, yellowed, or shifted in tone, we can apply a light color refresh before the new topcoat. This brings the wood back to the right tone without requiring full stain penetration into bare wood.

**Step 3: Fresh topcoat**
A new coat of polyurethane — oil-based or water-based depending on the application — applied over the prepared surface. Full protection, renewed sheen, no harsh chemicals required.

That's it. The existing base stays. The topcoat is new. The wood looks right again.

## SECTION: What surfaces are right for PUP.

**H2:** Doors, staircases, kitchens, architectural woodwork — anywhere the base is solid.

PUP works on any architectural wood surface where the existing finish is structurally sound. The base needs to be adhered — not peeling, not cracking, not lifting. The finish can be dull, lightly worn, or slightly faded. That's exactly what PUP addresses.

**Surfaces where PUP is commonly the right call:**

- **Doors** — Interior and exterior doors that are sound but have lost their sheen from cleaning and daily use
- **Staircases** — Handrails, newel posts, and balusters that are holding their finish but looking worn
- **Kitchen cabinets** — Cabinets with a solid existing base that are looking dull or lightly worn, not failing
- **Architectural woodwork** — Crown molding, door casings, wainscoting, built-ins that have a sound finish but need a refresh
- **Mantels** — Fireplace surrounds that are in good condition and just need to look current again

**PUP is not for:**

- Surfaces with peeling, cracking, or lifting finish — those need a full Strip & Refinish
- Surfaces with significant stain bleed, deep scratches, or structural damage
- Freestanding furniture — PUP is for architectural woodwork only

You don't need to make that call yourself. We assess every surface at the estimate and tell you honestly whether it's a PUP candidate or needs a full refinish.

## SECTION: Why "no harsh chemicals" matters.

**H2:** Full chemical stripping is sometimes unnecessary — and not always the right tradeoff.

Chemical stripping is the right method when the existing finish needs to come off. When it doesn't, it's an aggressive intervention that takes more time, creates more disruption, and isn't better for the wood just because it's more thorough.

For architectural woodwork in living spaces — the wainscoting in a dining room, the built-in bookcases in a study, the kitchen cabinets where food gets prepared — the ability to maintain surfaces without introducing chemical strippers into the home has real value. PUP gets the surfaces looking right again without it.

This is especially relevant in homes with children or anyone with chemical sensitivities. It's also faster: most PUP projects complete in days rather than weeks.

## SECTION: The distinction that matters.

**H2:** PUP is not a cheaper substitute for a full refinish. It's a different service for a different situation.

This is worth being clear about because it's where the most confusion happens.

PUP is not the budget option for surfaces that need a full refinish. If the existing finish base is compromised — peeling, cracking, flaking, lifting — a maintenance coat on top of it won't hold. The new topcoat will fail along with the old base.

PUP is the right service when the base is solid and the surface is a maintenance situation, not a restoration situation. The difference matters, and it's the first thing we assess at your estimate.

If your woodwork is a PUP candidate, we'll say so and tell you why. If it needs a full Strip & Refinish, we'll say that too. You won't be pushed toward a full refinish when PUP is actually the right call — and you won't be sold a maintenance coat on surfaces that actually need to be stripped.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: How is PUP different from a full refinish?**
A: A full Strip & Refinish strips the existing finish down to bare wood, repairs any damage, and applies a completely new stain and finish. PUP keeps the existing finish base and applies a fresh topcoat over it after wet sanding for adhesion. PUP is appropriate when the base is solid. A full refinish is appropriate when it isn't.

**Q: How do I know if my woodwork is a PUP candidate?**
A: You don't need to know — that's what the estimate is for. We assess each surface and tell you which service it needs. Signs that a surface may be a PUP candidate: the finish is dull or lightly worn but not peeling or cracking, the wood color looks faded but the base is adhered, the surface looks tired but structurally sound. If in doubt, call and describe what you're seeing. (314) 367-6054.

**Q: Does PUP work on painted surfaces?**
A: It can, for architectural woodwork where the painted finish is sound and well-adhered. The same criteria apply: if the base is solid, PUP may be appropriate. If the paint is peeling, cracking, or lifting, a full refinish is required.

**Q: How long does PUP last?**
A: A properly applied PUP coat adds significant life to a solid existing base. On doors, 5–8 years before another maintenance coat is typical with normal use. On kitchen cabinets with heavy use, 3–5 years. On architectural millwork with lighter traffic, longer. The key is starting with a sound base.

**Q: How long does a PUP project take?**
A: Significantly less than a full Strip & Refinish. Most single-room architectural woodwork PUP projects complete in 2–5 days. A kitchen cabinet PUP project typically takes 3–7 days depending on door count. We give you a specific timeline at the estimate.

**Q: Can PUP be done for furniture?**
A: No. PUP is for architectural woodwork only — surfaces built into the home. For freestanding furniture, we're not the right fit.
