### H2: What happens when a front door finish fails.

A failing front door finish isn't just an appearance problem. When the topcoat peels and cracks, moisture gets into the wood at the bottom rail, the jamb edges, and anywhere the finish has lifted. The wood swells and contracts with each rain cycle. The finish peels faster with each pass. Left unaddressed, what started as a cosmetic fix becomes a rot repair — or a door replacement.

In St. Louis, that timeline accelerates. South- and west-facing doors can see UV intensity that breaks down even quality exterior polyurethane within four or five years. A door facing west in Central West End, Compton Heights, or Lafayette Square gets afternoon sun year-round — not just in summer.

A properly refinished door with correct UV-resistant topcoat handles those conditions. The difference is in the prep, the product selection, and the cure environment — which is why we take doors to the shop.

### H2: The board-up method — why we take the door to the shop.

For most front door projects, we remove the door from its hinges, transport it to our shop, and refinish it in a controlled environment. Temperature and humidity stability during the cure phase produce a more consistent, more durable finish than on-site work in variable exterior conditions.

There are other advantages. No chemical smell in your home. No sanding dust in your entryway. The door is worked flat, which means finish applies and levels better than it does on a vertical surface in a doorway.

We typically have front doors back and rehung within **2–4 days**. You're given a temporary door or security arrangement while yours is in the shop. For doors that can't safely be removed — fixed sidelights, complex multi-panel assemblies — on-site work is available.

### H2: Is your door a candidate for Strip & Refinish or Perk Up & Protect?

Not every front door needs a full Strip & Refinish. If your door's finish is still structurally sound — not peeling, no bare wood exposed, just dull or faded — a **Perk Up & Protect** maintenance coat may be the right answer. Light sanding, color refresh, and a fresh topcoat. Faster turnaround, lower cost, no harsh chemical stripping.

**Strip & Refinish** (S&R) is the right call when:

- The finish has peeled, cracked, or lifted anywhere on the door
- There's bare wood exposed — especially at the base or edges
- The door has never been refinished and the original coat is decades old
- There's visible water damage or darkening at the bottom rail

**Perk Up & Protect** (PUP) is the right call when:

- The finish is intact but dull or faded
- Color is uneven but the surface is sound
- You want to extend the life of a recent refinish

When in doubt, we assess during the estimate visit and tell you which approach makes sense for your door.

### H2: Pre-sale front doors — a fast, high-return fix.

A worn front door is one of the most commonly flagged negatives in pre-listing walkthroughs. It photographs badly, it signals deferred maintenance, and it shapes the buyer's first impression before they step inside. A refinished front door, by contrast, signals that the home has been cared for.

We can typically complete a front door project in under a week, which fits most pre-listing timelines. If you're preparing to sell a home in Central West End, Clayton, Shaw, or Lafayette Square, this is worth doing before the photographer arrives.

### H2: EPA Certified — required for pre-1978 front doors.

The exterior woodwork on St. Louis homes built before 1978 almost certainly contains lead paint — including front doors, frames, and jambs. Stripping those surfaces without proper protocols generates lead dust at the entry of your home.

Sue Wheeler is an EPA Certified Lead Renovator. Every pre-1978 front door project includes documented containment, HEPA filtration, proper disposal, and written clearance. This isn't optional compliance language — it's how the work is done, every time.

**Q: How often does a refinished front door need attention?**
A: It depends primarily on sun exposure. A south- or west-facing door in full sun — common in Central West End, Lafayette Square, and Compton Heights — typically needs attention every 4–6 years. A covered or north-facing door in good condition can go 8–10 years. We'll assess yours and give you a realistic maintenance timeline.

**Q: Does the direction my door faces really change how fast it wears?**
A: Significantly. A west-facing door gets direct afternoon sun through the hottest part of every day. UV is the primary degradation mechanism for exterior wood finishes — it breaks down the binders in the topcoat, which leads to chalking and then peeling. A south-facing door has similar exposure. A north-facing or fully covered door lives a much easier life. We factor this into finish selection and maintenance recommendations.

**Q: Can you replace rotted sections of a front door?**
A: Minor rot at the bottom rail or door edge can often be stabilized and filled as part of a Strip & Refinish. More significant structural rot needs to be evaluated honestly — if the wood integrity is compromised, refinishing over it doesn't fix the underlying problem. We assess rot honestly and tell you when replacement is the better investment.

**Q: Do you refinish the door frame and sidelights too?**
A: We can, and usually recommend doing the frame when the door is being done. A freshly refinished slab next to a weathered frame or failing sidelights reads as incomplete. We quote the full entry — slab, frame, sidelights as applicable — and let you decide what scope makes sense.

**Q: I have a front door with glass panels. Does that change the process?**
A: Glass-panel doors require careful masking and hand prep around each lite. They can't go through a chemical dip tank, and the profiles around the glass need hand work to strip correctly. The board-up method still applies — we take the door to the shop. Timeline extends slightly due to the additional hand work. See our [French door refinishing page](/door-refinishing/french-door-refinishing/) for more detail on glass-panel doors.
