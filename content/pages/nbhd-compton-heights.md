## SECTION: Compton Heights homes and their woodwork

Developed between 1890 and 1920, Compton Heights sits adjacent to Tower Grove Park in one of St. Louis's most intact affluent historic neighborhoods. The homes here are primarily late Victorian, Colonial Revival, and Craftsman Foursquare — built on larger lots with an emphasis on interior quality that distinguished them from the row house neighborhoods nearby.

What sets Compton Heights woodwork apart is its scale and integration. The dining room built-in isn't just a hutch — it's a full wall piece with leaded glass uppers, plate rail, mirror back, and carved pilasters. The staircase newel post is thick enough to feel structural because it is. The library has floor-to-ceiling bookcases with fluted columns and crown detail. These were built as permanent features of permanent homes.

Compton Heights is a conservation district, and many homeowners here are deeply invested in preserving original character. They know the built-ins can't be replaced. They want them restored properly.

## SECTION: What we refinish in Compton Heights

Compton Heights projects tend toward the high end of scope. Homeowners here are working with elaborate original pieces and they want the result to match the quality of what was built.

**Built-in hutches and dining room buffets.** The dining room built-in is the signature feature of a Compton Heights home — and one of the most common calls we get from this neighborhood. Many have been painted white at some point, the quartersawn white oak underneath invisible under layers of latex over oil over shellac. We strip them by hand in place, repair any damage to the profiles, and refinish to match the surrounding flooring or millwork. The grain in these pieces — once revealed — is extraordinary.

**Built-in bookcases and library woodwork.** Library and living room bookcases in Compton Heights homes are often architecturally significant: leaded glass cabinet doors, fluted pilasters, dentil crown. Restoring them requires hand work throughout. Every component has its own finish condition, its own detail to preserve.

**Staircases.** Compton Heights staircases run from clean Craftsman designs to elaborate Victorian-influenced pieces with turned and carved spindles, heavy carved newels, and continuous railings. We do full-scope staircase restoration: treads, risers, every spindle, the newel post, and the railing — all stripped and refinished together so the result is visually consistent throughout.

**Architectural millwork and formal rooms.** The formal rooms in Compton Heights homes — parlor, dining room, entry hall — typically have a complete millwork package: door and window surrounds, baseboards, picture rails, possibly crown. When all of it needs work, we address it systematically so the finish matches across every piece in the room.

## SECTION: Restoring original woodwork vs. replacing it

Compton Heights homeowners occasionally ask about replacing original built-ins or millwork with new material. The honest answer is that it can't be done at the same level. Old-growth white oak and chestnut are not commercially available. The profiles on original Compton Heights millwork were milled locally in the late 1800s and early 1900s — custom profiles that no longer exist in any catalogue. The scale of the original pieces — their thickness, their weight, their proportion — reflects construction budgets and craft that simply aren't present in contemporary work.

Restoration starts from what exists and makes it better. The wood is already there, already dimensioned correctly, already aged into the house. A proper hand-strip and refinish reveals the original character and extends the life of the piece for another generation.

The only exception is genuine structural failure — rot or damage that compromises the piece. We'll identify that at the estimate and tell you honestly what it means.

## SECTION: Lead paint and pre-1978 homes in Compton Heights

The vast majority of Compton Heights homes predate 1978 — most predate 1930. Lead paint is present in original finish layers on virtually all woodwork. Every built-in, every staircase component, every piece of millwork in these homes should be treated as lead-containing until proven otherwise.

Sue Wheeler is EPA Certified under the Renovation, Repair and Painting rule. Proper containment, HEPA vacuuming, and documentation are standard on every Compton Heights project. This isn't optional compliance — it's the right way to protect your household during restoration work.

## SECTION: FAQ

**Q: My dining room hutch is gorgeous underneath the paint — I can see it at the edges. Can you match the original finish?**
Yes. Stain and finish matching is central to this kind of work. We test on a hidden area before committing to the full finish, and we'll show you samples at the estimate. The goal is for the piece to look as if it was never painted over.

**Q: Can you refinish a staircase while we're still living in the house?**
Yes. We work in sections so the staircase stays passable throughout the project. We typically do one side at a time — one bank of treads and the adjacent spindles — and alternate until the full scope is done. The schedule is worked out with you before we start.

**Q: I have original leaded glass cabinet doors on my built-in. Can those be preserved during refinishing?**
Yes. We remove the glass panels before any stripping or finishing and reinstall them after. We do not use chemicals that would harm the leading or glass. This is standard practice on Compton Heights built-ins.

**Q: How is your refinishing different from what a painter would do?**
A painter refinishes surfaces. We restore original woodwork — which means stripping back to bare wood, assessing and repairing the underlying material, and applying a finish appropriate to the piece and the era. The process is slower, more involved, and produces a result that looks like original workmanship rather than a painted surface.

**Q: Do you do kitchen cabinet refinishing in Compton Heights?**
Yes. Original kitchen cabinetry in Compton Heights homes is typically solid wood and worth restoring. We strip and refinish in place or remove doors and drawers to the shop depending on the scope.
