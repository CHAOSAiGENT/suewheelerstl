## Outline

### Intro (75 words)
- Open with the operator's reality: wood surfaces take daily abuse — heat, moisture, alcohol, cleaning chemicals, dragged glassware
- The smart operators don't wait for failure — they schedule maintenance
- Thesis: a maintenance contract is a business decision, not just a cleaning plan

---

### H2: What a Commercial Maintenance Contract Actually Covers (150 words)
- Annual or bi-annual inspection + condition assessment
- Light sand and fresh coat on surfaces showing wear before damage sets in
- Priority scheduling — you're not in the queue, you're on the calendar
- Fixed rate per cycle vs. emergency/reactive pricing
- Bullet list of typical coverage: bar tops, tabletops, wainscoting, booth backs, host stand, door frames

---

### H2: The Real Math — Maintenance vs. Full Refinishing (200 words)
- Full refinishing cost vs. maintenance coat cost (3–5 year intervals for a bar top that sees 200+ covers a night)
- When surfaces are maintained on a cycle, strip-and-refinish is often avoidable for 10–15+ years
- Walk through a scenario: 8-seat bar, high-traffic restaurant, 3-year maintenance cycle vs. neglect + emergency refinish
- "An ounce of prevention" is not a cliché when you're looking at the cost of shutting down your bar station for 3 days

---

### H2: Why Scheduling Matters as Much as the Work (150 words)
- Commercial operators can't just close for a week whenever wood shows wear
- A maintenance contract means Sue knows your calendar — work is booked during your slow week or off-hours
- Surfaces can sometimes be done in sections if traffic can't be redirected
- No emergency scrambling, no last-minute pricing, no arguing with a refinisher who doesn't know your space

---

### H2: What Accelerates Wood Wear in Commercial Settings (150 words)
- Cleaning chemicals (most commercial degreasers eat finish fast)
- Heat and steam near kitchen pass-throughs
- Alcohol spills left too long
- UV light through windows on bar-facing facades
- Consistent drag of glassware and plates across the same surface zones
- Practical note: knowing your accelerators helps schedule the right cycle length for your space

---

### H2: How to Know If Your Business Needs a Contract (150 words)
- You've been in business 3+ years and haven't refinished anything
- Staff uses commercial cleaning products directly on wood surfaces
- You've had a customer comment on the condition of the bar or tables
- Your finish is "working" but you can feel the roughness when you run a hand across the surface
- You're planning a slow season and want to use it productively
