## Outline

### Intro (100 words)
- Open with the reader's situation: they own or are buying a home in a St. Louis historic district — maybe CWE, Lafayette Square, Compton Heights — and the original woodwork needs real work
- The question nobody asks their refinisher: can I get money back on this?
- Thesis: federal and Missouri state tax credits can apply to interior woodwork as part of a qualifying rehabilitation — but the method matters

---

### H2: The Short Answer — Yes, If You Qualify (150 words)
- Federal Historic Tax Credit: 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures (QREs)
- Missouri Historic Tax Credit: 25% for most projects (35% for certain targeted areas)
- Combined: up to 45–55% of eligible costs returned as credits
- Key qualifier: the work must follow the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation
- Important: credits apply to income-producing properties primarily; residential rental and owner-occupied rules differ — introduce the distinction here, flesh out below

---

### H2: What Makes Wood Refinishing a Qualifying Expenditure (200 words)
- The Standards call for preserving and repairing original historic materials where feasible — not replacing them
- Refinishing (not replacing) original woodwork = preservation of historic fabric = QRE
- Hand-stripping specifically aligns with NPS guidance; chemical dip-stripping and sandblasting may not
- Examples of qualifying work: refinishing original doors, staircases, built-ins, millwork, window frames
- What does NOT qualify: replacing original wood with new material, using methods that damage historic fabric
- Note: individual project elements must be documented and approved — this is where your architect or SHPO liaison comes in

---

### H2: Federal vs. Missouri — Two Credits, Different Rules (250 words)
- **Federal 20%:** Applies to certified historic structures used for income-producing purposes. Owner-occupied primary residences do NOT qualify for the federal credit.
- **Missouri 25%:** Missouri's credit IS available to owner-occupied residential properties. This is a major distinction — most STL homeowners in historic districts can access the state credit.
- Missouri 35% tier: applies to projects in certain geographic areas or meeting additional criteria — worth asking your CPA or SHPO about your specific address
- Both credits require: National Register listing (or contributing structure in a listed district), approval of the rehabilitation plan by the State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO), completion of work that meets the Standards
- Claiming the credits: federal credit flows through tax return; Missouri credit may be transferable/sellable — this has significant value for investors

---

### H2: Which St. Louis Neighborhoods Qualify (150 words)
- National Register districts: CWE, Lafayette Square, Compton Heights, Tower Grove East/South, Shaw, Soulard, Maplewood, others
- Certified Local Historic Districts: add local overlay in some cases
- Not all houses in these neighborhoods automatically qualify — contributing vs. non-contributing structure distinction matters
- How to check: Missouri SHPO database, Landmarks Association of St. Louis, or ask Sue — she's worked in these neighborhoods for 36 years and knows what projects typically qualify
- Brief note: some West County suburbs have individual NR-listed structures (not district-level) — different path to qualification

---

### H2: The Method Has to Match the Standard (150 words)
- Even if your project qualifies financially, the work itself has to comply
- NPS Standard 6: Deteriorated historic features shall be repaired rather than replaced
- NPS Standard 2: Preserve the historic character — don't alter what makes it historic
- Hand-stripping preserves grain, detail, original profiles — aligns with both standards
- Chemical dip-stripping: raises grain, can loosen joints, may alter profiles — potential disqualifier
- Mechanical sanding beyond minimal: same risk
- This is why the refinisher you hire matters — not just the contractor, but the method they use

---

### H2: How to Start If You Think You Qualify (150 words)
- Step 1: Confirm your property's status (NR listed, contributing structure in a district, or individually listed)
- Step 2: Contact SHPO or a preservation architect before starting work — retroactive approval is not guaranteed
- Step 3: Document existing conditions with photos before any work begins
- Step 4: Get your refinisher's method on paper — what they use, what they won't use, why
- Step 5: Work with a CPA familiar with historic tax credits — the credit mechanism is real but requires proper handling
