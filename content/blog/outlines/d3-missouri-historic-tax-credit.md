### Intro (75 words)

- The Missouri Historic Tax Credit is one of the most robust state-level programs in the country
- And critically: it covers owner-occupied residential properties, which the federal credit does not
- Thesis: if you own or are buying a historic home in St. Louis, this program is worth understanding before you start any major rehabilitation work

---

### H2: The Basics — What the Missouri Credit Is (150 words)
- Administered by the Missouri State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) within the Department of Natural Resources
- Credit rate: 25% of Qualified Rehabilitation Expenditures (QREs) for most projects
- 35% rate available for: projects in certain distressed areas, projects utilizing specific workforce requirements, or projects meeting other targeted criteria (worth checking your address)
- Applies to: certified historic structures — either individually listed on the National Register or contributing structures within a listed historic district
- Key residential advantage: Missouri's credit is available to owner-occupied residential properties, unlike the federal 20% credit which is restricted to income-producing properties
- The credit is also transferable — it can be sold or assigned to other taxpayers, which creates value even for homeowners who may not have sufficient tax liability to use the full credit themselves

---

### H2: What Qualifies as a Rehabilitation Expenditure (200 words)
- Must be a Qualified Rehabilitation Expenditure (QRE) — work on the historic structure itself, not land or new additions
- Interior work on original historic materials qualifies: original woodwork, original plaster, original floors, original windows and doors
- Refinishing original wood surfaces: qualifying if the method aligns with the Secretary of Interior's Standards
- Replacing original materials generally does NOT qualify — even with historically accurate replacements
- The 5% threshold: for residential projects, QREs must exceed the adjusted basis of the property in some cases — a tax professional should confirm this for your situation
- Documentation requirement: before-and-after photos, contractor invoices, work descriptions — all need to be submitted to SHPO
- Timing: work must be approved before it starts in many cases; retroactive applications face significant hurdles

---

### H2: How to Apply — The SHPO Process (200 words)
- Part 1 Application: Confirm the property's historic significance — is it a certified historic structure?
- Part 2 Application: Submit the rehabilitation plan for SHPO review before work begins
- Part 3 Application: Submitted after work is complete — certifies that the work as executed followed the approved plan
- State credit certificate issued upon Part 3 approval; certificate can be applied to Missouri income tax liability or sold/transferred
- SHPO contacts and resources: Missouri DNR Historic Preservation Program (dnr.mo.gov)
- Timing note: the Part 2 process takes time — plan for this before project start, not after

---

### H2: Missouri Credit + Federal Credit — Can You Stack Them? (150 words)
- Yes, with conditions — the two programs can be combined on income-producing properties
- Owner-occupied residential: can use Missouri 25% only (federal 20% is not available)
- Rental properties and commercial historic properties: can potentially stack both credits
- For a project with $50,000 in QREs on a qualifying rental property: $10,000 federal + $12,500 Missouri = $22,500 in combined credits
- The stacking potential is a significant driver for real estate investors targeting historic districts — it materially changes renovation economics
- Even without stacking, the 25% Missouri credit alone is meaningful for a homeowner running $20,000–$50,000 in rehabilitation work

---

### H2: A Note on Wood Refinishing Specifically (150 words)
- Wood refinishing on original historic woodwork qualifies as a QRE when it meets the Standards
- Method matters: hand-stripping preserves historic fabric and is consistent with Standard 6 (repair rather than replace)
- Dip-stripping or aggressive mechanical methods may not be considered consistent with the Standards — and SHPO reviewers have discretion
- Finish selection: shouldn't dramatically alter the historic character of the wood
- Practical advice: get your refinishing scope documented before the Part 2 submission — it makes the SHPO review smoother
