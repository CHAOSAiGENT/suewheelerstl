### H2: The Variables That Drive Staircase Refinishing Cost (200 words)
- **Number of treads:** The primary count variable — a straight 12-tread flight is priced differently than a 20-tread switchback
- **Component scope:** Treads only vs. treads + risers + stringers vs. full scope including railings, balusters, and newel post — each adds time and material
- **Condition of existing finish:** Light wear vs. heavy paint build-up vs. prior DIY attempts vs. bare raw wood — condition dictates the prep process
- **Wood species:** Oak and maple respond differently than pine; some species require more careful stain control
- **Repair requirements:** Damaged treads, loose balusters, split newel posts — repairs are scoped separately and added to the base refinishing cost
- **On-premises vs. board-up:** Staircases can't be removed, so staircase work is always on-premises — the most labor-intensive delivery method
- **Existing finish type:** Lead paint (pre-1978 homes) adds EPA-required containment and disposal procedures to the cost

---

### H2: What Scope Looks Like at Different Levels (250 words)
Walk through three realistic scenarios — not price ranges, but scope descriptions:

**Minimal scope — treads only, good condition:**
- 12–14 treads, sound existing finish with normal wear, no repairs needed, no railings included
- Process: light sand, fresh polyurethane coat
- This is the PUP (Perk Up & Protect) equivalent — maintenance, not restoration

**Mid scope — full flight, mixed condition:**
- Treads + risers + stringers, moderate wear or older paint, minor repairs to one or two treads
- Process: chemical strip, sand, stain if needed, two coats polyurethane
- This is the most common residential staircase project Sue encounters

**Full scope — treads through newel post, heavy condition work:**
- Complete refinishing of treads, risers, stringers, railings, balusters, newel post; significant prep; color change or full strip
- Process: full Strip & Refinish on every component; possibly repair work on balusters or newel post
- This is the project that transforms a staircase; also the one that takes several days on-premises

### H2: The Lead Paint Factor (150 words)
- Most St. Louis homes built before 1978 have lead paint on original woodwork — including staircases
- Lead paint adds required protocol: containment, HEPA vacuuming, safe disposal of stripped material
- Only an EPA Certified Lead Renovator can legally perform this work in a way that protects the household
- The cost addition is real — but it's also not optional. An uncertified refinisher doing lead-safe shortcuts is a liability and a health risk.
- Sue is EPA Certified — this is standard on every pre-1978 job, not an add-on
